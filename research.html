Manuscripts Under Review

  1. Shen, Y., & Yao, H. Second chances, unequal trajectories: Adult higher education after vocational tracking in China.
    Abstract ↓Hide abstract ↑

    Adult higher education is often framed as a second chance for people disadvantaged by earlier educational sorting. This study examines whether vocational- and general-track graduates experience similar income mobility after completing the same level of adult higher education in China. Using survey data from 11,408 associate-degree graduates, we use ordered logistic regression and a series of robustness checks. Vocational-track graduates are 3.8 percentage points less likely to experience upward income mobility. They also devote less time to academic reading and independent study and show slightly smaller gains in lifelong learning capacity, whereas general and professional competency gains differ little between tracks. The mobility gap is concentrated among private-sector workers, graduates who remain with the same employer, and those with weaker field alignment. The findings show that inequality can persist after later educational attainment converges, as formally similar qualifications are converted into mobility under different learning and employment conditions.

  1. Xu, H., Shen, Y., Chen, G., & Yao, H. (in press). From access to learning gains: Motivational profiles, learning processes, and competence development among adult learners at an open university. Open Education Research. [In Chinese; CSSCI-indexed; CNKI Composite Impact Factor: 11.401.]
    Abstract ↓Hide abstract ↑

    Open universities have expanded adults’ opportunities to return to higher education, but educational access does not necessarily translate into learning gains. Drawing on survey data from 9,880 graduates in the 2024 cohort of a large public open university in eastern China, this study uses Illeris’s comprehensive theory of learning as an analytical framework and applies latent class analysis and regression analysis to examine adult learners’ enrollment motivation profiles, learning processes, and competence development. The results identify four groups of adult learners: routine enrollees, flexibility-oriented learners, professionally oriented learners, and broadly affirmative learners. The four groups differ markedly in learning participation, supportive learning environments, and learning constraints. Flexibility-oriented learners face more pronounced time constraints; professionally oriented learners show advantages in professional competence and lifelong learning competence; and broadly affirmative learners report stronger development across competence dimensions. After learning-process variables are included, differences in competence development across motivational profiles narrow substantially, while interactive learning participation and supportive learning environments show particularly stable associations with competence development. The findings suggest that open universities should not only expand educational opportunities but also pay closer attention to heterogeneous learner needs, strengthen instructional interaction and process support, and facilitate the translation of educational opportunities into substantive competence development.

  2. Shen, Y., Wu, Y., Na, X., & Wang, A. (2023). The impact of agricultural mechanization on the efficiency of agricultural carbon emissions in China—Based on the Super-SBM-Tobit model. Environmental Ecology, 5(10), 53–61. [In Chinese; 31 citations in CNKI, October 2026.]
    Abstract ↓Hide abstract ↑

    With the proposal of the “dual carbon” goal, carbon reduction has become an important task for agricultural development. In order to help China's agricultural low-carbon development from the perspective of agricultural mechanization and explore its impact on China’s agricultural carbon emission efficiency, based on the panel data related to agricultural production in 31 provinces (cities, autonomous regions) of China from 2011 to 2020, comprehensively used the Super SBM model and Tobit model to calculate the carbon emission efficiency of China's provincial agriculture, and focused on analyzing the impact of agricultural mechanization level on agricultural carbon emission efficiency. The research results indicate that: The average agricultural carbon emission efficiency in China from 2011 to 2020 is 0.925, with an average annual growth rate of 2.744%. The overall agricultural carbon emission efficiency is steadily improving, but there is still significant room for improvement, and there are significant differences among regions; The level of agricultural mechanization has a positive correlation with agricultural carbon emission efficiency at a significant level of 1%. Improving the level of agricultural mechanization development, promoting the synergy between agricultural production development and energy conservation and emission reduction, is the only way for China to move towards the goal of “dual carbon” under the New Normal in the New Era. Based on the above conclusions, the article proposes suggestions from four perspectives: technological innovation, adapting to local conditions, policy support, and building agricultural mechanization service organizations.

  3. Wu, Y., Shen, Y., Xu, S., & Liang, R. (2023). Study on coupling coordination development of agricultural production efficiency and carbon emissions in Yangtze River Economic Belt. Acta Agriculturae Jiangxi, 35(6), 149–157. [In Chinese; 10 citations in CNKI, October 2026.]
    Abstract ↓Hide abstract ↑

    Based on the panel data of 11 provinces and cities in the Yangtze River Economic Belt from 2011 to 2020, this paper measured agricultural production efficiency and carbon emission levels, and analyzed the spatial and temporal changes of the coupling coordination degree and the migration trend of its equilibrium point in the Yangtze River Economic Belt by the coupling coordination degree and the migration model of the center of gravity. The results show that the overall coupling coordination level of Yangtze River Economic Belt is high, and the improvement of production efficiency and carbon emission level is synchronous. The spatial trend of production efficiency, carbon emission level and coupling coordination degree is increasing from southwest to northeast and from inland to coastal. During 2011-2020, the agricultural production efficiency and coupling coordination degree of the Yangtze River Economic Belt show a trend of first decreasing and then increasing, with the fastest rate of the upper reaches, while the agricultural carbon emission intensity shows a trend of fluctuating and decreasing, with the fastest rate of the upper reaches. The difference of coupling coordination degree between regions is decreasing, and the upstream gradually catches up and overtakes the middle and lower reaches. The coupling coordination center of agricultural production efficiency and carbon emission level in the Yangtze River Economic Belt is located at the junction of Hunan and Hubei, which migrates from northeast to southwest and from coastal to inland. According to the above research results, we propose to prioritize the development of upstream areas, formulate policies according to local conditions, and firmly implement policies.

Selected Working Papers

  1. Shen, Y., Wu, Y., & Yao, H. Do University Excellence Initiatives Transform Cities? Entrepreneurship, Innovation, and Urban Economic Development in China. [In Chinese]
    Abstract ↓Hide abstract ↑

    Taking the first round of the “Double First-Class” university initiative as a quasi-natural experiment, this study draws on panel data from 285 Chinese cities over the period 2007–2022 and, grounded in regional innovation systems theory, systematically examines the impact of high-level university development on urban entrepreneurship, innovation, and high-quality economic growth. The results show that the “Double First-Class” initiative significantly enhances the level of high-quality economic development in cities, a finding that remains robust across multiple robustness checks. Moreover, the initiative exerts differentiated effects on entrepreneurial activity in traditional manufacturing versus emerging industries, promoting high-quality economic growth by catalyzing industrial transformation and upgrading. At the same time, high-level university development facilitates a shift in the mode of economic development by strengthening industry–university–research collaborative innovation. Heterogeneity analyses further indicate that this quality-enhancing effect is jointly constrained by cities’ resource scarcity and population absorption capacity, exhibiting characteristics of both “timely assistance in adversity” and “adding luster to success.” Based on these findings, the study recommends strengthening governmental coordination and differentiated support to amplify the regional multiplier effects of the “Double First-Class” initiative; guiding firms to become deeply embedded in industry–university–research systems to consolidate an innovation-driven industrial foundation; and promoting the connotative development of universities alongside forward-looking disciplinary layout to reinforce the fundamental underpinnings of high-quality development.

  2. Shen, Y., Chen, S., Cheung, A. C. K., & Chen, Y. Beyond performance gains: A three-level meta-analysis of the effects of generative AI on EFL learners’ motivation, emotion, and self-efficacy.
    Abstract ↓Hide abstract ↑

    Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) is increasingly embedded in English as a foreign language (EFL) instruction, yet evidence on its psychological effects remains fragmented across motivation, emotion, and self-efficacy. This three-level meta-analysis synthesized 90 effect sizes from 41 (quasi-) experimental studies to estimate GenAI’s effects on these outcomes separately while accounting for dependent effects within studies. GenAI-supported instruction produced significant favorable effects on motivation (g = 0.793, p < .001), emotion (g = 0.569, p < .001), and self-efficacy (g = 0.782, p < .05). Substantial heterogeneity was attributable to between-study variance, while none of the 23 omnibus moderator tests identified significant moderators across learning context, intervention design, or research design. Descriptively, effects were consistently larger with substantive human support, while pedagogical functions showed different profiles across outcomes: feedback was largest for motivation, tutoring/scaffolding for emotion, and conversation/interaction for self-efficacy. These findings indicate that GenAI can support distinct dimensions of EFL learners’ psychological experience, while broad contextual and design categories do not yet reliably explain variation in effect magnitude. More fine-grained attention to how GenAI functions within particular learning activities may better clarify when and how these psychological benefits emerge.

  3. Chen, S., Cheung, A. C. K., Shen, Y.*, & Ho, S. M. Beyond the hype: A three-level meta-analysis on the effects of artificial intelligence on university students’ self-regulated learning. (* Corresponding author)
    Abstract ↓Hide abstract ↑

    Despite rapid growth in research on the effects of artificial intelligence (AI) on self-regulated learning (SRL), meta-analytic evidence remains scarce. This three-level meta-analysis demonstrates that AI has a small but significant positive effect on university students’ SRL (g = 0.34). Although the moderator analyses did not reveal statistically significant between-group differences, several descriptive trends merit attention. AI appeared to be more beneficial for cognitive engagement (g = 0.40), metacognitive knowledge (g = 0.39), the performance phase (g = 0.33), and self-reflection (g = 0.39), while its effects on motivational beliefs, resource management, and the forethought phase remained limited. AI also appeared to show relatively stronger positive effects in programming (g = 0.34). In terms of AI application, adaptive systems and personalization (g = 0.34), as well as intelligent tutoring systems (g = 0.39), yielded significant but small effects, whereas assessment and evaluation did not. Moreover, significant effects were observed only for standardized measures (g = 0.37), not for non-standardized measures. Significant effects were found in classroom settings (g = 0.35), but not in lab or online settings, and only studies conducted in Eastern regions produced significant effect sizes compared with those conducted in Western regions. These findings underscore the need for more design-sensitive AI-SRL interventions and warrant cautious interpretation of generalized claims regarding AI’s transformative impact on SRL.
